RESEARCH INTERESTS

Research

My research focuses on two complementary streams:

Supplier-Directed Prosocial Behavior

I study how buying firms develop empathy toward suppliers and translate that understanding into supportive actions. This research emphasizes the importance of supplier perspectives, constraints, and capabilities in building more responsible and sustainable buyer–supplier relationships.

Supply Chain Transparency

I examine how firms disclose their upstream supplier relationships and how the extent of disclosure relates to supply-chain-specific reputational exposure. My research distinguishes between the decision to disclose supplier relationships and how much of the observable upstream network a firm makes public.

JOB MARKET PAPER

Let It Out or Hide It All! The Reputational Cost of Partial Supply Chain Transparency

Authors: Parisa Rajabi, Robert D. Klassen, Kenneth K. Boyer, and Christian Blanco

Abstract

Using large-scale buyer–supplier relationship and reputational risk data, we examine whether transparency is necessarily beneficial for firms. Our findings suggest that the decision to begin disclosing upstream supplier relationships is associated with increased reputational exposure, while greater disclosure coverage among transparent firms is associated with lower exposure. The findings highligh tthe importance of moving beyond the question of whether firms disclose to understanding how much they disclose.

OTHER DISSERTATION PAPERS

Buying Firm Empathy and Supplier-Directed Prosocial Behavior in Buyer–Supplier Relationships

Authors: Parisa Rajabi and Robert D. Klassen

Abstract

This conceptual study introduces buying-firm empathy into the literature on buyer–supplier relationships. Drawing on cognitive and affective empathy, we examine how buying firms can understand suppliers' perspectives, constraints, and goals, and how this understanding may motivate supplier-directed prosocial behavior. The study develops theoretical propositions and identifies conditions under which empathy-informed buyer behavior may support more responsible supplier relationships.

Interpreting Buyer–Supplier Relationships through Cognitive Empathy and Supplier-Directed Prosocial Behavior

Authors: Parisa Rajabi, Robert D. Klassen, and Jury Gualandris

Abstract

This study uses exploratory qualitative multiple-case research involving 13 firms in the Canadian agri-food sector to examine how buying firms develop cognitive empathy toward suppliers and translate their understanding into concrete supportive practices. The findings identify how buyers come to understand suppliers' operational realities and how empathy-informed practices can emerge within buyer–supplier relationships, particularly when suppliers offer differentiated capabilities and share broader goals with buyers.

Working Papers

Transparency Trajectories in Buyer–Supplier Networks

This project extends my job market paper by examining how firms' upstream disclosure patterns evolve over time, whether distinct transparency trajectories emerge, and how those patterns relate to supply chain outcomes.

Supplier-Directed Prosocial Behavior: An LLM-Based Measure from News and Media Analytics

This project develops an approach to identifying observable supplier-supportive actions in news and media sources using LLM-assisted text analysis. It builds on insights from my qualitative research, with the goal of measuring supplier-directed prosocial behavior on a larger scale.

Buying Firm Empathy and Supplier Outcomes in Buyer–Supplier Relationships

This project develops a scenario-based experimental design to investigate how empathy-informed responses from buying firms may influence suppliers' commitment and opportunistic behavior.

Information Sharing and Cost Reduction in Green and Regular Supply Chains under Government Tariffs

This analytical research project uses game-theoretic modeling to examine information sharing and cost-reduction decisions in supply chains under government tariffs.
